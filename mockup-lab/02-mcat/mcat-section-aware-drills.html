What this shows

Identical on both sides: the session spine, drill counter, timer, origin card (section · cause · source · attempt), submit + automatic scheduling, keyboard shortcuts, and "talk it through". One component set, one queue, one screen.

The C/P drill is a calculation — real numbers, given relationships, no calculator. Distractors are the specific wrong answers your error would produce, which is what makes it remediation rather than a generic question.

The CARS drill has no passage, and that's the interesting part. §2a forbids generating CARS passages — so instead of faking one, the drill trains the answer-choice pattern in isolation. Recognising extreme language is the actual skill, and it doesn't need a passage to practise. Cheaper to build and more targeted.

No Again / Hard / Good / Easy. Self-grading is for open recall, where only you know how well you produced the answer. A multiple-choice drill has already scored you — so the outcome drives the interval: wrong → soon · right but flagged/guessed → medium · right and unflagged → longer. Intervals are shown, not chosen. Mastery is clearing it correctly and unflagged across two spaced encounters, never a self-rating.

Compare to what was rejected: three separate practice screens with their own runners, empty states, and navigation. This is a template difference behind one screen.
