Behaviorism — 6 things to hit.

  1. Core claim — psychology should abandon the study of consciousness and study only observable behavior
  2. Justification — the scientific method rests on verifiability; you cannot see or touch a thought
  3. John B. Watson — father of behaviorism; hard nurture, behavior is fully governed by the environment
  4. B.F. Skinner — internal mental states exist but cannot be studied scientifically
  5. Skinner's principle — repeat what produces positive outcomes, drop what produces negative → reinforcement and punishment
  6. Free will — an illusion, because behavior is fully governed by external stimuli
Extra

John B. Watson, not James — your handwritten notes and the auto-transcript both say James. James Watson is the DNA scientist.

A patient means to say “I love my mother” and instead says “I hate my mother.” What is this called, and what did Sigmund Freud take it as evidence of?

A Freudian slip — evidence that the unconscious influences behavior from below the level of awareness.

Extra

This is the shape of Freud's whole argument: he could not observe the unconscious, so he argued from the places it leaks out. Dreams were his other route.

Rank B.F. Skinner, Sigmund Freud, and Carl Rogers on how much freedom they grant a person, and say what each thinks actually drives behavior.

Skinner — none; external stimuli and reinforcement history. Freud — little; unconscious conflicts below awareness. Rogers — most; the person's own self-concept and drive to grow.

Axis — how much freedom the person is granted

Extra

Freud and Skinner both take control away from the conscious person, but from opposite directions — inward for Freud, outward for Skinner.
