No labels, on purpose. An instruction, a statement of fact, and someone else's words are told apart by shape — a chip and a sentence, a ruled statement, display type in quotation marks. Andy: “users just want to read info.” The type still exists in the record; it drives the shape and the filters, and is never printed.

The pillar chip is the way in. Selection shuffles across every pillar, weighted toward the ones you open least, so the material you are avoiding is the material most likely to surface. The weight is invisible and the copy never mentions it — no “you haven't opened Letters in a month.” It just shows up.
