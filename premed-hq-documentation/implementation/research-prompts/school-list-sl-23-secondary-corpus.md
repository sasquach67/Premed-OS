# Research packet — School List `SL-23`: secondary-prompt corpus feasibility

**Question.** Could a very small team honestly maintain a per-school medical-school secondary-prompt corpus, rather than relying on the already-specced student-paste flow?

**Scope.** This packet evaluates publication, freshness, and reuse constraints. It does **not** collect, reproduce, or endorse any actual prompt text. It is evidence for a later product ruling, **not** a product ruling.

**Accessed:** 2026-08-13

## Maintenance verdict

**Verdict: not maintainable honestly by one person doing one annual pass. Confidence: medium-high.**

The deciding issue is not merely copyright. Official sources show the live workflow is individual-school invitation/portal distribution, while the accessible compilations are third-party collections with update claims rather than an official, per-entry, source-stamped feed. A safe corpus would require, for every school and each cycle:

1. confirming whether the school sends a secondary and to whom;
2. obtaining the current question set after that invitation/portal event;
3. recording the cycle, source URL or attributable submission, character limits, and check date for every question;
4. revisiting entries when a school changes its portal or question set; and
5. withholding any item whose source or cycle cannot be verified.

That is an ongoing, distributed collection and verification operation. An annual page-level pass cannot prove freshness for questions that may appear or change inside portals during the cycle. A maintainer could build a **student-contributed, cycle-stamped** system only if it ships with provenance, a staleness rule, moderation, and a fail-safe omission path; this packet does not establish that such a system should be built.

## Evidence-backed implications — non-binding

1. The currently specced `SL-23` phase-1 flow—student pastes the actual prompt when the secondary arrives—has the strongest freshness property: the student is looking at the current source.
2. A future shared corpus, if ever reconsidered, should not present a prompt as current unless it carries a cycle, provenance/source, and visible verification date. It needs a strict “unverified/withhold” state rather than a plausible old prompt.
3. General prompt **archetypes** are a different, lower-maintenance research object from school-specific, current prompt text. This packet does not evaluate or authorize them.
4. No provider’s public update claim is sufficient evidence to import its corpus into the app or `med-schools.json`.

## Explicit non-findings and limits

- No official source in this pass quantified the share of schools that publish prompts in advance; therefore this packet deliberately gives **no percentage**.
- No official or academic source quantified year-over-year prompt changes across U.S. medical schools.
- No public structured/licensable current-secondary-prompt feed was found from AAMC, AACOM/AACOMAS, TMDSAS, or a school consortium.
- MedCoach and GradPilot names require source disambiguation before either can be counted as a medical-secondary corpus provider.
- This packet does not decide whether phase 2 is cut, deferred, or built; it does not alter `SL-23`, the student-paste flow, or any app code.
