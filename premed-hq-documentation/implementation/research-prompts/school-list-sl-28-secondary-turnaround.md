# Research packet — School List `SL-28`: secondary turnaround window

**Question.** Is there a defensible recommended turnaround time for a medical-school secondary, or should School List show only elapsed time since receipt?

**Scope.** School admissions pages, AAMC, and institutional pre-health advising sources. This is evidence for a later product ruling, **not** a product ruling. It does not authorize a countdown, a target, an attention severity, or a status change.

**Accessed:** 2026-08-13

## Findings

| Source class | Finding | Strength / limit |
|---|---|---|
| **School admissions page** | UNLV's Kirk Kerkorian School of Medicine says secondaries should be completed within **14 days** of invitation and publishes that alongside its 2026–27 calendar. | A genuine school-level expectation; it cannot be generalized as every school's deadline. |
| **School admissions bulletin** | Medical College of Georgia's published bulletin says Regular Decision applicants must submit the secondary within **two weeks** of receipt; its historic Early Decision rule was one week. | Shows an institution can set a concrete rule, but the indexed bulletin is older and must never be treated as current-cycle data. |
| **Institutional pre-health offices** | UConn, Maryland, Duke, Northwestern, UCSD, UVA, and University of Washington each advise about **two weeks**; Northwestern gives a one-to-two-week range. | Strongly corroborated advising norm, but not a service rule or an outcome study. |
| **AAMC** | AAMC says secondaries are school-specific and applicants should wait to be formally invited. Its public guidance reviewed here does **not** publish a universal number of days for return. | The official service source supports the per-school distinction, not a global target. |
| **Outcomes evidence** | No published admissions-office dataset or peer-reviewed study was found in this pass that quantifies the causal effect of submitting within 7, 14, or any other number of days. | Do not convert the norm into a claim that a particular day count changes odds. |

## Primary-source evidence

### School-level instructions

- [UNLV School of Medicine — Admission Selection Process and Timeline](https://www.unlv.edu/medicine/admissions/selection-process) — current indexed 2026–27 calendar: “Secondary applications should be completed within two weeks (14 days) of invitation.” Accessed 2026-08-13.
- [Medical College of Georgia admissions bulletin](https://www.augusta.edu/mcg/admissions/documents/admissions-mcg-bulletin-3.28.23.pdf) — published school bulletin: Early Decision returns within one week; Regular Decision secondary and required documents within two weeks after receipt. **Historic source only; not current-cycle data.** Accessed 2026-08-13.

### AAMC

- [Advisor Corner: Preparing for Secondary Applications](https://students-residents.aamc.org/applying-medical-school/advisor-corner-preparing-secondary-applications) — AAMC describes secondaries as school-specific, says schools vary in how/when they send them, and instructs applicants to wait until formally asked to submit. It does not set a universal turnaround duration. Accessed 2026-08-13.
- [Applying to Medical School](https://students-residents.aamc.org/applying-medical-school/applying-medical-school) — AAMC describes a secondary as an additional school-specific application with school-varying fees and required forms. Accessed 2026-08-13.

### Institutional pre-health guidance

- [University of Connecticut — Secondary Applications](https://premed.uconn.edu/secondary-applications/) — advises completing secondaries within two weeks; notes distribution timing varies and some schools use response time as one indicator of interest. Accessed 2026-08-13.
- [University of Maryland Health Professions Advising — Application Process](https://prehealth.umd.edu/application-process) — its cycle timeline says to complete and submit secondaries within two weeks of receipt. Accessed 2026-08-13.
- [Duke Prehealth — Applying in Summer 2025](https://advising.duke.edu/prehealth/apply/MedSchool2026M/) — advises submission within two weeks of receipt and distinguishes automatic, post-processing, and screened secondary distribution. **Historic cycle page; advice is not a current deadline.** Accessed 2026-08-13.
- [Northwestern Health Professions Advising — Primary vs. Secondary Applications](https://www.northwestern.edu/health-professions-advising/pre-health-paths/pre-medicine/applying/primary-vs.-secondary-applications/) — advises one to two weeks after receipt. Accessed 2026-08-13.
- [UC San Diego Pre-Health — Apply](https://prehealth.ucsd.edu/get-started/apply.html) — advises “ASAP (within two weeks)” and notes question types vary. Accessed 2026-08-13.
- [University of Virginia Career Center — Secondary Applications FAQ](https://career.virginia.edu/faq-secondary-applications) — recommends within two weeks and notes applicants can receive several at once. Accessed 2026-08-13.
- [University of Washington Pre-Med Application Guide](https://www.washington.edu/uaa/prehealth/site/assets/files/1133/premedapplicationguide.pdf) — advises returning secondary responses within two weeks of receipt. Accessed 2026-08-13.

## Where “two weeks” comes from

This pass found the rule repeated across independent institutional advising offices and at least one school-level current instruction. It did **not** identify a single originator, a service-level rule, or an empirical study establishing fourteen days as a universal threshold. The evidence supports calling it a **widely used advising norm**, not a fact about all schools or an outcome guarantee.

## Evidence-backed implications — non-binding

1. An `SL-28` row may safely state the measured fact, for example **“Secondary received 9 days ago.”**
2. If a school-specific deadline was entered or pasted by the student, the product may compute time until that deadline; that is a fact about the student's own record.
3. A generic “two-week” target can only be shown as sourced **general guidance**, never as a hard deadline, lateness verdict, or probability claim. It must yield to a school-specific deadline.
4. The safest no-ruling interim is elapsed time with no target. A student who has not entered a deadline should not see a false precision countdown.

## Explicit non-findings and limits

- No AAMC, AACOM, or TMDSAS universal secondary-return window was found.
- No causal admissions-outcome study was found. Statements that rolling admissions favours early completion are advice, not proof that day 14 changes an applicant's outcome.
- The packet does not decide notification severity, target wording, or whether `SL-28` ships.
- Secondary deadlines and invitations are cycle- and school-specific; the product may not backfill them from this research.
