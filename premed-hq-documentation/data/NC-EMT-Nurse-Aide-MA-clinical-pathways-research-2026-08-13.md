# North Carolina clinical-role pathways near UNC: EMT, Nurse Aide I/CNA, Medical Assistant, and local EMS service

**Research date:** 2026-08-13  
**Scope:** North Carolina regulator/Board sources; current official Durham Technical Community College, Wake Technical Community College, UNC Health Careers, and South Orange Rescue Squad pages.  
**Purpose:** A source-of-truth routing packet for Premed OS/Atlas. It describes the lawful credential/training sequence and currently published local routes. It does **not** rank roles by prestige, tell a student which role to choose, promise a job, or turn a course’s outcome statement into a state credential.

## Executive answer

| If a student is asking about… | Stable official route | What becomes true only after that route | What Atlas must not imply |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **EMT** | Complete an NC OEMS-approved initial EMT educational program → meet the state’s initial credentialing conditions/examination route → hold the issued NC credential before representing oneself as an NC EMT | The person may seek roles whose *live posting* accepts their actual credential; an EMS agency can separately set onboarding/affiliation requirements | Completing class = an NC credential; a credential = a job, agency affiliation, permission to perform every possible procedure, or a UNC Campus EMS place |
| **Nurse Aide I** (often called “CNA”) | Complete a state-approved Nurse Aide I training-and-competency route **or** qualify for another DHSR examination/reciprocity route → pass both competency-exam portions → be listed on the NC Nurse Aide I Registry | The person holds an active registry listing, subject to renewal/work requirements | “CNA” is a North Carolina professional license; a course completion alone = registry listing; every hospital job accepts a NA I listing |
| **Medical Assistant (MA)** | North Carolina does **not** license or regulate MAs as a distinct occupation. An employer determines its education/credential preferences and delegates only appropriately supervised lawful tasks | A student can apply to a live MA opening with the education/training it actually requires; a formal MA program is one preparation route, not a state gate | MA is a North Carolina state license; any MA may perform a fixed statewide task list; a national MA credential authorizes tasks without employer/licensed-provider delegation |
| **Local volunteer EMS service** | Hold the EMT credential specified by the service, then complete that service’s membership/onboarding process | Membership/service activity after the organization accepts and trains the applicant | An EMT course automatically places the student on an ambulance or with Campus EMS/SORS |

**Vocabulary boundary:** In North Carolina, official DHSR materials call the registry category **Nurse Aide I**. “CNA” is a common job-market shorthand, but it should be stored/displayed as a user-facing alias—not as a claim that NC issues a nursing-assistant license. Wake Tech’s official syllabus specifically says nurse aides are not licensed and work under RN supervision.

## 1. EMT in North Carolina

### Credential sequence

1. **Choose an OEMS-approved educational institution/course** at the EMT level. North Carolina’s current rules require completion of an approved educational program for initial credentialing; the Office of EMS (OEMS) is the state credentialing and EMS-education authority. The OEMS education manual also identifies entry criteria that include being 17 by course end and a high-school diploma/equivalency or an 11th-grade-or-higher reading assessment; the provider must confirm its own current admissions process. [NCOEMS Credentialing](https://oems.nc.gov/credentialing/) · [NCOEMS Education Program Manual](https://oems.nc.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/NCOEMS-Education-Program-Manual-121323-2.pdf)
2. **Complete the course’s required didactic, laboratory, clinical/field, and skills components.** The exact calendar, prerequisites, health screening, background review, attendance rule, and cost come from the individual educational institution—not a universal state schedule.
3. **Use the applicable NC credentialing/testing route.** North Carolina’s initial-credentialing rule requires the approved education, a scope-of-practice performance evaluation, and an OEMS-administered or OEMS-approved equivalent written examination. The rule states the credential is not issued until age 18, though an examination may be taken at 17. The current codified rule is the authority if an older archived PDF differs. [Current 10A NCAC 13P rules](https://reports.oah.state.nc.us/ncac/title%2010a%20-%20health%20and%20human%20services/chapter%2013%20-%20nc%20medical%20care%20commission/subchapter%20p/subchapter%20p%20rules.html)
4. **If already nationally registered or credentialed in another state, assess legal recognition instead of assuming an initial-course pathway.** NCOEMS says legal recognition requires either NC residence or affiliation with an NC EMS provider, plus a current valid NREMT or eligible other-state credential; its page explicitly says an applicant whose initial education was completed at an NC OEMS-approved school may not use that particular legal-recognition path. [NCOEMS Legal Recognition](https://oems.nc.gov/legal-recognition/)
5. **Maintain the credential through its current renewal route.** The NC rule sets a four-year credential term and applies renewal conditions separately; renewal/eligibility details, including background-review requirements, must be checked directly with OEMS/Continuum for the individual credential. This does not replace the separate NREMT renewal status a student may also hold.
6. **Only then apply to a specific employer or volunteer service using its live requirements.** Do not turn a state credential into an assertion of agency affiliation, a paid job, or a student-organization seat.

### What the state rule does—and does not—settle

- **Established:** initial credentialing involves approved education, performance evaluation, and written examination; NCOEMS administers credentialing/testing/legal recognition. It can conduct compliance/background processes. [Rule .0502](https://info.ncdhhs.gov/dhsr/rules/2016/1230_13P_0502.pdf) · [NCOEMS FAQs](https://oems.nc.gov/faqs/)
- **Not converted into product data:** a universal “EMT scope checklist.” Actual permitted care is governed by the student’s credential level, current NC/OEMS rules, medical direction, and the authorized service’s protocols. Atlas should link to the authority and store the user’s real credential/agency, not present a generic skills list as permission.
- **Not established here:** a fixed statewide tuition, course length, job availability, pay, number of shifts, or a guaranteed NREMT-vs-NC testing sequence. Those vary by provider and posting.

## 2. Nurse Aide I / “CNA” in North Carolina

### Registry sequence

1. **Use a state-approved Nurse Aide I Training and Competency Evaluation Program (NAT/CEP) or a state-approved Competency Evaluation Program (CEP), unless a DHSR-approved alternate eligibility route applies.** DHSR says federal rules set a 75-clock-hour floor for approved training, while North Carolina can require more. [DHSR education requirements](https://ncnar.ncdhhs.gov/naedureq.html)
2. **Pass the written and skills portions of the competency examination within the stated timing/attempt limit.** For a person who completed an NC state-approved training program, DHSR says both portions must be passed within two years of training completion or within three attempts, whichever comes first. [DHSR eligibility and training waivers](https://ncnar.ncdhhs.gov/natrainingwaiver.html)
3. **Be placed on the NC Nurse Aide I Registry.** The registry is the state’s listing for people who have met applicable training/competency requirements; verify active status rather than relying on a course certificate. [DHSR listing and re-listing](https://info.ncdhhs.gov/dhsr/hcpr/floaide.html)
4. **Maintain the listing.** DHSR says it is valid for two years; to maintain it, the aide must work for pay as a nurse aide under RN supervision at least eight hours during the two-year period, then submit the required renewal/employment-verification forms. If the minimum work requirement is not met for two full years, re-testing is required. [DHSR listing and re-listing](https://info.ncdhhs.gov/dhsr/hcpr/floaide.html)
5. **Use a live employer posting to determine whether a particular job accepts NA I, requires a specific patient-care-tech role, requires BLS, or adds other screening/onboarding conditions.** Registry listing is not a universal hospital-hiring guarantee.

### Alternate routes that must not be hidden

- **Reciprocity:** DHSR describes an approved reciprocity route for an aide in good standing on another state’s registry; it is not a blanket transfer. [DHSR listing and re-listing](https://info.ncdhhs.gov/dhsr/hcpr/floaide.html)
- **Training-waiver/competency-exam eligibility:** DHSR lists certain other eligibility categories, including people with a current unencumbered EMT or paramedic credential, subject to the state’s application and exam requirements. This is an *exam-eligibility* pathway, not an assertion that EMT equals NA I or that an employer must hire the person as a nurse aide. [DHSR eligibility and training waivers](https://ncnar.ncdhhs.gov/natrainingwaiver.html)
- **Employer verification:** DHSR states health-care providers must check the registries before hiring unlicensed health-care personnel. That is an employer duty, not a student’s substitute for a live job requirement. [DHSR registry verification](https://info.ncdhhs.gov/dhsr/hcpr/flonaidv.html)

### Scope/legal boundary

- The NC Board of Nursing classifies nurse aides/CNAs as **unlicensed assistive personnel (UAP)** when they provide delegated care. UAP works under delegation/direction from authorized licensed providers; it does not possess an independent statewide nursing scope. [NCBON UAP guidance](https://www.ncbon.com/uap-general-information)
- Wake Tech’s official syllabus says nurse aides are not licensed and work under RN supervision. It is a useful local translation of the legal boundary, but the Board/regulator controls the rule. [Wake Tech NA I syllabus](https://www.waketech.edu/sites/default/files/program-page-files/Nurse-Aide-I-Syllabus-05042026.pdf)
- Therefore Atlas may say: **“Nurse Aide I Registry listing; care duties are delegated/supervised and employer-policy-specific.”** It must not render a generic independent “CNA scope” checklist.

## 3. Medical Assistant (MA) in North Carolina

### State/legal route: there is no NC MA license

The NC Board of Nursing says MAs/MOAs are UAP and that, even if nationally certified, they are **not regulated and/or licensed under occupational laws and rules in North Carolina and do not have a designated scope of practice**. The NC Medical Board likewise says delegation to an unlicensed worker depends on qualifications, legality/custom, and appropriate physician supervision. [NCBON office-practice delegation statement](https://www.ncbon.com/sites/default/files/documents/2024-03/ps-office-practice-setting.pdf) · [NC Medical Board delegation guidance](https://www.ncmedboard.org/RESOURCES-INFORMATION/professional-resources/publications/forum-newsletter/articles/delegating-medical-tasks-to-unlicensed-personnel)

**Correct decision sequence:**

1. Read the *specific live employer posting* for its required/preferred education, national certification, BLS, experience, and schedule conditions.
2. If pursuing formal preparation, evaluate a program’s curriculum, clinical placement, accreditation, time, and tuition as an education choice—not as an NC licensing requirement.
3. Once hired, perform only tasks the supervising licensed provider/employer has lawfully delegated under current policy and training.

**Feasibility implication (not prestige):** the reviewed formal MA options are multi-term programs, unlike a student simply applying to an employer’s MA opening. That is a time/eligibility distinction only. Which route is viable depends on the live employer requirement, the student’s schedule, financial situation, and desired work—not on a universal “best pre-med role” hierarchy.

## 5. Paid work: official current route, intentionally no static openings

The official [UNC Health Careers portal](https://jobs.unchealthcare.org/) is the owner route for paid UNC Health roles. Its contents are dynamic. This packet intentionally does **not** label any MA, NA I/CNA, EMT, patient-care technician, scribe, observer, or clinical-research job as open because a title search without an individually verified live posting can quickly become wrong.

### Required display rule for a job card

Only create a job record after reading its live official posting. Preserve:

```ts
type ClinicalJobLead = {
  employer: string
  title: string
  facility: string
  employmentType: string | null
  credentialRequirementsVerbatim: string[]
  otherEligibilityVerbatim: string[]
  postingUrl: string
  postingId: string | null
  observedOpenAt: string
  sourceCadence: 'live_posting'
  status: 'open_at_observation' | 'closed_or_removed'
}
```

Never fill this from a search-engine summary, a course page, or an old copy. A course/certification packet can route a student to the official careers search but cannot declare them qualified or employed.

## 8. Deliberate gaps and next verification questions

1. **EMT renewal/CE detail:** NC’s current rule establishes a four-year state credential term, but this packet does not hard-code a continuing-education credit target or calculate renewal eligibility. The user’s issued credential and NCOEMS/NREMT are authoritative; Premed OS may hold an expiry and point to the authority.
2. **Live local jobs:** no individual opening is retained. Query the official UNC Health Careers portal at the moment the student asks.
3. **UNC Campus EMS:** requirements/application status require a stable official owner page or direct confirmation. Do not use Reddit/social snippets as product data.
4. **SORS acceptance/selection:** the site says applications are accepted, not that every applicant is admitted. Treat interview/cadet steps as selection/onboarding, not an open job queue.
5. **Course costs/clinical paperwork:** even the current official pages distinguish tuition from immunizations, CPR, background check/drug screen, uniforms, exams, and fees. Display those as provider-specific, date-stamped values—not generic cost estimates.
